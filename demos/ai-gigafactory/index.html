The AI Gigafactory

Where electricity, water and silicon are turned into intelligence. Scroll to take the tour, from the grid connection down to a single chip.

01 · The campus

A power plant in reverse

Hundreds of megawatts flow in from the grid. What leaves is heat, and tokens. Data halls, a substation, cooling yards and a solar field, sized for on the order of a hundred thousand accelerators.

02 · Cooling

Every watt becomes heat

Practically all the power a GPU draws ends up as heat. AI racks now draw well over 100 kW each, far more than air can carry away. So the coolant goes straight to the chips.

SupplyReturn / heat
03 · The hall

Hundreds of racks, one machine

This hall holds 560 racks. During training they behave like a single computer, and every GPU waits for every other one at each step.

04 · The rack

One rack, 72 GPUs

Current flagship racks wire 72 GPUs into one shared memory domain. Each weighs well over a tonne and draws as much power as a few hundred homes.

05 · Lights out

Switch off the daylight

…and you see what actually moves: electrons, heat and data. From here on, everything that carries a signal glows.

06 · Silicon

Two dies, eight memory stacks

One GPU package: two compute dies stitched together, flanked by stacks of high-bandwidth memory. Roughly 200 billion transistors, all doing one thing extremely fast: multiplying matrices.

07 · The network

Thousands of GPUs, one computer

Every rack connects to a leaf switch, and every leaf to the spine above it. After each training step, every GPU shares what it has learned with all the others. The network is as critical as the chips.

Inside a rack
≈ 1.8 TB/s per GPU
Between racks
≈ 400 – 800 Gb/s per GPU
08 · Training

Weeks in lockstep

Compute, then share. Compute, then share. Millions of times, while the loss, the model's error, slowly falls. One broken GPU stalls everyone.

Click any rack to make it fail.
Warming up…
09 · Output

Tokens out to the world

Everything in the building exists for this: a stream of tokens leaving Luxembourg at the speed of light, answering questions a continent away.
