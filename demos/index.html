Demos

Small interactive pieces, mostly about questions I got curious about outside work. Each runs in your browser and keeps whatever you enter on your device.

  • Sheet music · 4 scores

    Overscored

    Four nursery rhymes given far more score than they asked for, in the manner of Zimmer, Williams, Satie and Wagner. The score follows the music.

    More about Overscored

    None of the four is a set of variations. Itsy Bitsy Spider runs at four speeds at once. The Wheels on the Bus is cut into passengers who board one at a time until the tune is whole. Au clair de la lune waxes and wanes through one lunar month. Alle meine Entchen turns out to be a round, which lets it sit on a single bass note for 136 bars.

    Press play and a marker follows the score bar by bar; tap any bar to jump there. The first page of each score explains its form. The music was written by Claude at my prompting and the recordings are synthesised.

  • Scroll explainer · Physics

    The Quantum Computer

    One scroll from the lab into the refrigerator, down to a single qubit and the junction inside it, then out to a hall full of them.

    More about The Quantum Computer

    A 3D tour of a superconducting quantum computer. The shields come off the refrigerator to show its six plates, each colder than the last, and the camera zooms without a cut from the room to a chip, one qubit and a junction a fraction of a thousandth of a millimetre wide. Then the lights go out and the signals glow. It starts as a white architectural model and turns into a blueprint; you can switch between the two looks at any time.

    Watch the heat noise thin out from one plate to the next, steer a qubit on its sphere and measure it, and click or tap qubits to flip them and watch the error correction catch them, until you flip too many at once. Best on a computer; it runs on a phone too. The figures are illustrative, as of late 2026.

  • Scroll explainer · Infrastructure

    The AI Gigafactory

    One scroll through the building where AI is trained: from the grid connection and the cooling down to a single GPU die, then out to the rest of the world.

    More about The AI Gigafactory

    A 3D tour of an AI data-centre campus. The roof lifts off to show the cooling, the camera zooms without a cut from the whole hall down to the two dies of one GPU, then the lights go out and the network fabric, a training run and finally a globe of outgoing tokens light up. It starts as a white architectural model and turns into a glowing blueprint; you can switch between the two looks at any time.

    Switch between liquid and air cooling, turn labels on or off, and during the training run click or tap any rack to make it fail and watch the whole hall stall and roll back to its last checkpoint. Best on a computer; it runs on a phone too. The figures are illustrative.

  • Exploded views · 3 machines

    Exploded Views

    Three engines taken apart in 3D: an airliner jet, James Watt’s steam engine and a radial aircraft engine. One click switches between them.

    More about Exploded Views

    Each plate is a working model. Pull it apart with a slider, run it, open it in a cutaway or switch to a blueprint drawing, and click any part for a short explanation. Plate 01 is a two-spool turbofan with the airflow coloured from cold to hot. Plate 02 is Boulton & Watt’s rotative beam engine of the 1780s, with its parallel motion and sun-and-planet gear. Plate 03 is a nine-cylinder radial whose cylinders glow by stroke as the firing order runs round the star.

    Drag to turn the view, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click or tap a part. It runs on a phone, but a larger screen shows more. The models are illustrative: the figures follow the engines of today’s single-aisle airliners, the 1788 Lap Engine in the Science Museum, London, and the Pratt & Whitney R-1340 Wasp of 1925.

  • Game · 1 to 4 players

    Tabletop Turbo

    Toy cars race across the furniture, as in the tabletop racers of the 1990s. Thirty-six miniature worlds, four players on one keyboard.

    More about Tabletop Turbo

    A homage to the tabletop racing games of the 1990s, where tiny toy cars race across the furniture and four friends crowd around one keyboard. Thirty-six miniature worlds in nine cups, from a breakfast table and a garden to a model railway, a Mars diorama, a pinball machine, a haunted dollhouse and the open works of a watch, plus a Grand Duchy Cup that tours Luxembourg: a Moselle wine tasting, the Schueberfouer at night, a model of Grund and Clausen and a jigsaw of the Old Town. They are raced in cars, rovers, speedboats, hovercraft, snowmobiles, monster trucks, luxury SUVs and bumper cars, each with its own engine sound, and every world has its own music. Each world has its own hazards: spilt jam and ink, whirlpools, level crossings, Bunsen flames, trapdoors and turning gears. In head-to-head mode the screen follows the leader, and whoever drops off its edge is out. There are also lap races, cup championships with trophies, and a time trial against your own ghost car.

    Best on a computer. Players use the arrow keys, WASD, IJKL and the numpad, and the keys can be changed in the menu. With three or four players, “Easy keys” needs only two keys each. Gamepads work too, and on a phone you can race alone with touch steering and brake buttons.

  • Map · Ancient history

    Roman News Isochrones

    How many days did a message need to cross the Roman Empire? Pick a city, a messenger and a season.

    More about Roman News Isochrones

    How many days did a message need to cross the Roman Empire? Pick a starting city, a messenger and a season, and the map colours the Empire by travel time. Tap a city to see the fastest route by road and sea. The model is checked against journeys recorded by Cicero, Plutarch, Tacitus and Pliny.

  • Field app · Birding

    AndroBird 2026

    A bird-sighting recorder rebuilt for today’s phones: camera bearing, voice entry, survey walks and eBird export.

    More about AndroBird 2026

    The same idea, rebuilt with what a phone can do in 2026. Raise the phone and the bearing comes from the camera, corrected for magnetic declination. Say or type “3 red kites flying over” and the form fills itself in. It records survey walks with a GPS track, uses the European breeding codes, can blur the nest sites of sensitive species, and exports to eBird, GPX and GeoJSON as well as MiniAvi and CSV.

    In English, German and French. On a computer, open the Sightings tab and load the example data, a morning survey walk at Haff Réimech. It keeps its sightings separate from the 2012 version.

  • Field app · 2012 port

    AndroBird (2012)

    The Android app my brother and I built in 2012 for recording bird and amphibian sightings, ported to the browser.

    More about AndroBird (2012)

    In 2012 my brother and I built AndroBird, an Android app for recording bird and amphibian sightings. This is the same app rebuilt for the browser. Point your phone at the bird, estimate the distance, pick the species, and AndroBird places the sighting on the map using GPS and the compass. It covers more than 1,400 species with German names and exports to MiniAvi and CSV. You can install it on a phone and it works offline.

    The app is in German, like the original. It works best on a phone with GPS and a compass. On a computer, set the direction by dragging the compass, or long-press the map to add a sighting. Your sightings stay in your browser; only the map tiles are loaded from OpenStreetMap or Esri.
